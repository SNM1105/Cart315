Reflective Reading Response: Schön & Bennett: Reflective Conversation with Materials
Schön argues that design is not the execution of a pre-existing plan. Instead, design is a “conversation with materials”, where iterations lead to the final product. The designer asks questions through actions, such as sketches, code, and prototypes. The materials answer back through the results of these actions. Understanding comes about during the act of creating, and before it.

The most provocative idea in Schön’s work is the concept of “Backtalk”. We often treat unexpected results in design as errors to be fixed. Schön suggests we treat them as information. When materials “Talk Back”, they reveal hidden complexities that our initial mental model missed.
In my own work, I do typically rush to fix mistakes I make, but there are times where I create unexpectedly fun glitches / features. A great example would be one of the first games I ever made using JS. It was a game where you walk around a map and try to collect pages while avoiding these monsters. After every page is collected, more pages would spawn, the monsters would change location, and the movement speed would increase. There was supposed to be a win screen, but it never popped up, so after completing a room, it would just instantly throw you into the next level, which forced you to act quick. My play testers loved it and competed to get the highest score.

Schön dismantles the romantic notion of the designer as a solitary genius who visualizes the perfect solution in their mind and then executes it. He argues that there is no direct path between the designer’s intention and the outcome. The intention evolves through the act of design.
This is incredibly true. In all art, whether it be video games, painting, graphic designing or music, things change throughout the creative process. Lyrics change, instruments, colours change, stories change. I love the method of figuring it out as I go. It makes it inevitable to make mistakes and to learn from those mistakes. It also allows me to discover new and interesting things. This applies to all forms of media. Trying out new instruments, new forms of painting, new tools in photoshop or blender, new website ideas. I can’t learn if I don’t try, and I won’t get creative if everything goes too smoothly.
